## 0. Perception fix: big Mario (commit c3b4162, before any arm)
`senses_v2.mario_box(ram)`: 12 x 16 small, **12 x 24 when 0x0756 >= 1** (`knobs.MARIO_BOX_BIG`, fixed, registered). Measured in the emulator (scripted power-up poke, `tests/test_senses_v2.py`): collision box 12 tall small, 24 tall big, feet reference 0xCE = 176 for both. Used for: the eye point (hum, smell) and the head-side gap of every threat. The cleared-enemy test and the face / tower "feet above top" tests are feet- and width-based: they never used his height, so there was nothing to change there. Neutral while small: the 636-neuron brain with the new code reproduces S3e frame for frame. No arm episode ever became big (nobody ate the mushroom), so the big box was not exercised by the arms. (Caught on the way: a name clash in `look` overwrote `box` in the smell branch; fixed before any run, 92 flyrun tests pass.)

**Small-Mario assumptions still in the code (listed, not fixed):**
1. `HUM_REACH = 64`: the reach of a small Mario's ground jump; big Mario's head reaches ~8 px higher, so blocks at 64-72 px would be reachable but are silent.
2. `MAX_JUMP_HEIGHT = 68` px apex: measured with small Mario; it caps the pit tower; big Mario's apex was not measured.
3. Box width 12 for both, while the RAM collision box is 10 px wide small and 12 big (small Mario's width is over-estimated by 2 px; big matches).
4. `ENEMY_FEET_OFFSET`, the enemy sizes and the scripted driver's hold times were all measured with small Mario.
5. Big Mario's head meets the brick row at a different height of the jump (O0: bump at feet 153 vs 139 for a block underside at 128), so the block-jump geometry in the notes is small-only.

## 2. Regression: arm A on the 881-neuron brain vs S3e
Hum off, smell off, 10 HOLDOUT episodes: **9/10 flags, 1 death = pit 1 at x 1128 (noop 11, sigma 0.05): the same flags and the same cause on every episode as S3e.** Not frame-identical: 9 of 10 traces differ from S3e at some frame (first differences: noop 3/0.0 none, 11/0.0 frame 1228, 25/0.0 1242, 33/0.0 1250, 41/0.0 1258, 3/0.05 237, 11/0.05 161, 25/0.05 1012, 33/0.05 346, 41/0.05 355). Diagnosis (episode 11, sigma 0): at frame 1228 TTMn is 0.1003 vs 0.0998 around the release threshold 0.10, so A is released one frame apart; the 636-neuron brain with the same code is frame-identical to S3e, while the 881 brain moves TTMn by up to 0.0022 even with hum and smell off. **Accepted by decision (option 1):** the 881-neuron arm A is the baseline; the differences are single-frame release jitter from the GF-row renormalisation and, at sigma 0.05, a different noise draw order with more neurons. Rule recorded in SPEC.md ("regression = same flags / causes, not frame-identical, when the brain changes").

## Paragraph
Adding the hum made the fly better at the thing it was built for (the ?-blocks) and worse at the game: 1.9 blocks hit instead of 0.9 and the mushroom block opened in 20/20, but flags fell from 9/10 to 0/10 and the mean max_x from 2958 to 1220. The hum is a second input into the same Giant Fiber, so it lowers the looming angle at which he jumps from ~44 deg to 9-23 deg: five early jumps per run, four harmless and one at x 1201 into a Goomba that stands on the brick row above the mushroom block. Adding the smell on top changed nothing measurable in this level: the mushroom is behind him when it appears, the smell takes the steer to -0.0091 (not -0.01) and home wins, so there are no Left presses to speak of, 0 eaten, 0 big; the big-Mario perception was never exercised by an arm. The good: the hum hits the blocks by itself in full-level play, nothing fires with nothing in view, and the early jumps are exactly what the hum predicts. The bad: the hum is not scaled against the loom decision, so at a block next to an enemy the jump is mistimed. Nothing was changed (no rule, no gain); the HUM_GAIN / looming balance, a smell strong enough to beat home, and the hum's reach are what evolution would have to find.
